# Changas Brand System

## Purpose

Changas should feel energetic, friendly and accessible without turning every surface into a promotional gradient. The logo carries the strongest emotional load, so the product UI gives it room to stand out.

## Core palette

- Canvas: `#FFF9F3`
- Ink: `#202124`
- Brand orange: `#FF6B35`
- Brand yellow: `#FFC857`
- Functional blue: `#2563EB`
- Logo pink accent: `#FF0A78`

Accessible semantic derivatives are allowed when the exact brand color does not provide enough contrast for small text:

- Orange foreground: `#C84010`
- Warning foreground: `#9A6500`

## Composition rule

Use an approximate 70 / 20 / 10 hierarchy:

- 70% warm neutral surfaces: canvas, white and warm surface tones.
- 20% warm brand energy: orange, yellow and the logo gradient.
- 10% functional contrast: blue, semantic success/danger and small utility accents.

## Color roles

### Orange

Primary action color. Use for main CTAs, active progress, emphasized selection and important brand moments. Buttons use the exact orange with dark ink text for contrast.

### Yellow

Support color. Use for warm highlights, empty states, tips and low-intensity emphasis. Do not use the exact yellow for small foreground text.

### Blue

Functional contrast. Use for links, focus states, informational selection and secondary interaction cues. It should balance the warm palette rather than dominate it.

### Pink

Logo and attention accent only. Use sparingly for small notification counters or branded decorative details. It is not a general-purpose primary color.

### Neutral surfaces

`#FFF9F3` is the app canvas. Elevated cards use a near-white warm surface so the product remains calm and readable while the logo and CTAs retain visual impact.

## Gradient usage

The Changas gradient is reserved for brand/emotional surfaces:

`#FFC857 → #FF6B35 → #FF0A78`

Good uses:

- App icon and identity mark
- Hero or onboarding brand panel
- Avatar/brand placeholder where appropriate
- Splash or promotional surfaces

Avoid using it as a permanent background for forms, long reading surfaces, every card, or secondary buttons.

## Typography

The visual language uses a friendly sans display treatment rather than the previous editorial serif direction. Strong headings should feel rounded and direct; body copy remains neutral and highly legible.

## Interaction hierarchy

- Primary CTA: orange background + dark ink text.
- Secondary CTA: warm near-white surface + subtle neutral border.
- Links and focus: blue.
- Active workflow progress: orange.
- Informational active state: blue.
- Success: green semantic token.
- Warning: yellow-tinted background with darker amber text.
- Danger: semantic red.

## Mobile navigation

The bottom navigation stays visually quiet. The active destination uses a warm orange tint and orange foreground. Blue remains available for hover/focus and secondary utility states. Notification badges use the logo pink accent sparingly.

## Accessibility

Exact logo colors are not automatically valid foreground colors. Small text uses accessible derivatives where needed. Keyboard focus is blue and visually explicit. Primary buttons use dark ink over orange rather than white because the exact brand orange does not provide sufficient normal-text contrast with white.
